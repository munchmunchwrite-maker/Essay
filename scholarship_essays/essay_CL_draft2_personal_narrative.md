# HAUS Scholarship Essay — Draft 2
## Program: MSc in Computational Linguistics, Heidelberg University
## Angle: Personal Narrative / Language Journey
## Target: 500–600 words

---

The moment I understood that I loved language was not in a classroom. It was sitting in my room in Utah, reading German poetry I had found in a secondhand book, working through the words with a dictionary and an enormous amount of patience. Something about the density of German — the way compound words carry entire concepts, the way a line break can redirect an argument — made me feel that learning this language was not just acquiring a skill but entering a different way of thinking about the world.

That instinct never left me. It led me to volunteer teaching German to elementary students, to living with a Gastmutter in Germany during my Congress-Bundestag fellowship who spoke only German and who, through the patience of our halting daily conversations, gave me more linguistic insight than any coursebook could. It also led me, eventually, to see that my undergraduate training in Information Systems was not separate from this love of language but potentially in conversation with it. My coursework in Python, database systems, and AI for Business Processes raised questions I could not answer with a business degree: how do computational systems represent meaning? What is lost when language is processed automatically? What does it mean for a machine to understand a sentence?

The MSc in Computational Linguistics at Heidelberg University is where I intend to answer those questions. Heidelberg is one of the oldest and most distinguished research universities in Europe, and its Computational Linguistics faculty has particular strengths in natural language processing and corpus analysis — precisely the areas where I want to develop expertise. The city itself is exactly the kind of environment where I expect to do my best academic work: intellectually active, genuinely beautiful, and affordable in ways that make sustained academic focus possible. Studying there would also allow me to continue developing my German, which I believe is essential not just for proficiency but for genuinely engaging with German academic culture and intellectual life.

I am applying for the HAUS Scholarship because the financial support would remove the most immediate practical obstacle I face. As a recent graduate self-funding my postgraduate education, the $7,000 award would allow me to settle into Heidelberg — into the rhythms of a new country, a new language of instruction, and a new field — without the additional pressure of a first-semester financial gap. I also value the HAUS community specifically. The expectation that recipients will serve as ambassadors reflects a philosophy about study abroad that I share: that the value of the experience multiplies when it is shared with others.

My study proposal centers on natural language processing, with particular interest in how computational models can better account for pragmatic and culturally embedded meaning — the layer of language that my Gastmutter and I struggled to share even when our vocabulary was sufficient. My technical background in Python, database management, and applied data analysis will support the quantitative core of the degree. In the long term, I see this research orientation feeding into work at the intersection of language technology and international communication, in multilingual institutions or international organizations where the stakes of miscommunication are real and consequential.

As a HAUS Ambassador, I would return to Utah and Washington, D.C. with specific audiences in mind: University of Utah students who may not consider German universities as postgraduate destinations, and professionals in policy and technology circles who should understand how American researchers benefit from European academic environments. I would share the experience honestly and concretely, because I believe Heidelberg offers something genuinely rare, and the strongest ambassadorship is a specific and truthful account.
