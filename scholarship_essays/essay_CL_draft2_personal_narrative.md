# HAUS Scholarship Essay — Draft 2 (Revised)
## Program: MSc in Computational Linguistics, Heidelberg University
## Angle: Personal Narrative / Language Journey
## Target: 500–600 words

---

When I was eight years old, I could finally read. For most children that is not a milestone worth noting, but for me it was the result of years of work — I had a speech impediment and ADHD, and for a long time my teachers genuinely believed I might not catch up. The moment I became literate, something shifted permanently. I have not stopped reading since. By the time I was a teenager in Utah, I was working through German poetry from secondhand books with a dictionary and more patience than most people my age could summon. I am applying to Heidelberg University to pursue the MSc in Computational Linguistics because that early stubbornness about language eventually grew into a research question I cannot put down: how do computational systems account for what language actually does — the layers of meaning that live underneath and around the words themselves?

My undergraduate studies in Information Systems at the University of Utah gave me the technical tools to approach that question seriously: Python, database systems, business analytics, and a capstone in AI for Business Processes. My year in Berlin as a U.S. Department of State fellow through the Congress-Bundestag Programme gave me the clearest possible evidence that the question matters. Living with a Gastmutter who spoke only German, I discovered daily that translation tools fail precisely where language is richest — in humor, warmth, irony, and the cultural references that a native speaker draws on without thinking. I want to spend the next two years understanding why that happens and what can be done about it.

I chose Heidelberg specifically because of the people doing that work there. I have been following Dr. Letitia Pârcălăbescu's YouTube channel, AI Coffee Break with Letitia — she completed her PhD in Heidelberg's Computational Linguistics department — for over a year. Watching her explain genuinely difficult ideas in NLP with intellectual rigour and real enthusiasm made me realize that this department trains researchers who take the complexity of language seriously. That is not something I found when I looked at other programs, and it shaped my decision directly.

The HAUS Scholarship would make this possible in a way that matters beyond the numbers. I come from a single-parent household — my mother raised four children after leaving an abusive marriage and has worked as an accountant for as long as I can remember, doing everything she could to hold things together. She always told me her biggest regret was never studying abroad. I am the first person in my family to attempt graduate study in another country, and I am doing it without financial support from my institution or my family. The $7,000 award represents the difference between a first semester where I can focus fully on my work and one where I am managing a financial gap I cannot easily close. It would also, in a real sense, fulfill something my mother never had the chance to pursue — and that matters to me deeply.

My study proposal focuses on the two-year MSc in Computational Linguistics, with research concentration in NLP and how models can better represent pragmatic and culturally embedded meaning. Long-term, I aim to work in multilingual international contexts — institutions where the precision of language technology has real stakes. As HAUS Ambassador, I would return to Utah and to the D.C. networks I developed through my fellowship and internships and speak about Heidelberg honestly and specifically, because I believe the strongest advocacy for this university is a real account from someone who was actually there and found it worth the effort.
