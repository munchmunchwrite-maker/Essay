# HAUS Scholarship Essay — Draft 5 (Revised)
## Program: MA in Transcultural Studies, Heidelberg University
## Angle: Personal Transformation / Cultural Disorientation
## Target: 500–600 words

---

I could not speak English until I was eight years old. I had a speech impediment and ADHD, and for years my teachers weren't sure I would catch up. When I finally did — when language became something I could actually use rather than something that kept failing me — I became fascinated with it in a way I still haven't outgrown. I read everything I could find. I started teaching myself German from poetry in secondhand books. I volunteered with immigrants and refugees at the YWCA in Salt Lake City, where I could empathize with how hard it is to operate in a language you haven't fully claimed yet. Language, I understood early, is not just communication. It is access. I am applying to Heidelberg University to pursue the MA in Transcultural Studies because I want to examine that understanding at a graduate scholarly level — and because Heidelberg is the specific institution where I believe I can do that most rigorously.

What brought me to Heidelberg's program is the Heidelberg Graduate School for the Humanities and Social Sciences. I discovered its podcast, "Us and Them," while researching programs, and spent several evenings working through episodes. The way it approaches questions of identity, belonging, and cultural difference — not as soft social topics but as genuinely complex scholarly problems — gave me a clear sense of the intellectual culture at Heidelberg. That is where I want to be.

My year in Germany as a U.S. Department of State fellow through the Congress-Bundestag Programme gave me the sharpest version of the experience I now want to understand academically. I had studied German informally for years before arriving in Berlin, so I believed I was prepared. What I had not prepared for was how much of what I thought of as simply "how things are" turned out to be specifically American. Navigating institutional culture, professional expectations, and daily social life in Germany required something more than language study — it required recognizing my own assumptions and learning to hold them more loosely. Transcultural Studies is the discipline that gives that experience a framework, and Heidelberg is where I want to develop it.

My undergraduate degree in Information Systems from the University of Utah — GPA 3.912 — gives me the research discipline and analytical foundation that graduate work demands: structured problem-solving, data interpretation, and the ability to communicate complex findings clearly. My study abroad at the University of Waikato in New Zealand confirmed that I can adapt to an unfamiliar academic environment and perform well in it. Two years of policy-oriented data analysis at the U.S. Department of Commerce developed the research independence I will bring to the MA curriculum.

I am applying for the HAUS Scholarship because I am self-funding this degree, and the financial gap is real. My mother raised four children alone — she worked as an accountant for as long as I can remember, holding things together after leaving an abusive marriage, and always told us her one regret was never studying abroad. I am the first person in my family to attempt graduate study in another country, and I am doing it without institutional or family financial support. The $7,000 award would cover the gap of my first semester and allow me to begin the program with the stability that academic focus requires. It would also make possible something my family never had the means to pursue, which is not something I take lightly.

As HAUS Ambassador, I would return to Utah and to Washington, D.C. — where I developed professional networks through my Department of Commerce internship and Congress-Bundestag fellowship — and speak honestly about what Heidelberg offers. I would share what the intellectual environment is actually like, what the city is, and what it means to pursue this kind of graduate work as a first-generation American student studying abroad. I would also support future HAUS applicants directly, because I believe mentorship from someone who has done it is more valuable than any brochure.
