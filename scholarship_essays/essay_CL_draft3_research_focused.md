# HAUS Scholarship Essay — Draft 3 (Version 3)
## Program: MSc in Computational Linguistics, Heidelberg University
## Angle: Research-Focused / Intellectual Question
## Target: 500–600 words

---

The question that led me to Heidelberg came from a specific moment. During my year in Germany as a U.S. Department of State fellow through the Congress-Bundestag Programme, I lived with a Gastmutter who spoke only German. My undergraduate background in Information Systems, including Python, database systems, and AI coursework, didn't help much at the kitchen table. What struck me wasn't that I lacked vocabulary. It was that the translation tools I relied on early in the year failed consistently at exactly the moments that mattered: when she was being ironic, when a reference was culturally specific, when the emotion of a sentence lived in its structure rather than its words. I want to understand why that happens and how it can be changed. I'm applying to Heidelberg University to pursue the MSc in Computational Linguistics because it's the program best positioned to help me do that.

I chose Heidelberg specifically because of the work being done there. Dr. Letitia Pârcălăbescu completed her PhD in Heidelberg's Computational Linguistics department and runs the YouTube channel AI Coffee Break with Letitia, which I've followed for over a year. Her ability to engage rigorously with questions in NLP while keeping the implications for real communication genuinely in view gave me a clear sense of the department's intellectual culture. That's the environment I want to learn in. Heidelberg's strengths in corpus linguistics and language modeling, within one of Europe's most distinguished research institutions, make it the right institutional home for the questions I want to pursue.

My undergraduate degree in Information Systems from the University of Utah gives me the technical entry point: Python, SQL, business analytics, and a capstone in AI for Business Processes. My study abroad at the University of Waikato in New Zealand, where I earned an A+ in Applied Computing Tools, confirmed that I can adapt to a different academic system and perform well in it. Two years of applied data analysis at the U.S. Department of Commerce, working with large Census datasets under real time pressure to support federal policy decisions, developed the research rigor and independence that graduate work requires. My proposed program of study is the two-year MSc in Computational Linguistics, with a focus on NLP and how models can better account for pragmatic and context-dependent meaning. Long-term, I want to apply this expertise in international or multilateral institutions, where cross-linguistic precision has real institutional consequences.

I'm applying for the HAUS Scholarship because the financial gap is genuine, and I want to be upfront about that. I grew up in a single-parent household. My mother raised four children alone, working as an accountant after leaving an abusive marriage, and she gave me both the discipline to pursue ambitious goals and the awareness that pursuing them takes real support. She always talked about regretting that she never got to study abroad. I'm the first person in my family to attempt it at the graduate level, and I'm doing it without institutional or family financial backing. The $7,000 award would reduce first-semester costs meaningfully and allow me to begin my studies with the stability and focus that research-level work demands.

As HAUS Ambassador upon my return, I'd reach out to University of Utah students and to the professional networks I developed through my Congress-Bundestag fellowship and Department of Commerce internship in Washington, D.C. I'd document my experience in writing and photographs, participate in HAUS chapter events, and speak about Heidelberg from genuine, specific experience, because I think that kind of concrete advocacy is what actually moves people to consider a destination they hadn't imagined.
