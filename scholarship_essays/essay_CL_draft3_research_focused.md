# HAUS Scholarship Essay — Draft 3
## Program: MSc in Computational Linguistics, Heidelberg University
## Angle: Research-Focused / Intellectual Question
## Target: 500–600 words

---

My path to computational linguistics runs through three countries and two fields. A Bachelor of Science in Information Systems from the University of Utah gave me fluency in Python, relational databases, and applied data analysis. A year in Germany as a U.S. Department of State fellow with the Congress-Bundestag Programme introduced me to the lived complexity of language: the gaps between what translation tools produce and what a native speaker communicates, the cultural weight carried by words that do not cross cleanly into English. The MSc in Computational Linguistics at Heidelberg University is where I intend to bring these two trajectories together in a rigorous and sustained research program.

The specific questions I want to pursue involve how natural language processing systems can better model pragmatic and context-dependent meaning — the dimension of language most responsible for the translation failures I observed during my fellowship. When I lived with a German-speaking Gastmutter who spoke no English, I quickly learned that vocabulary alone was insufficient. There were moments of humor, warmth, and frustration that moved faster than my ability to parse them, and no translation tool captured what they contained. My technical background gives me the tools to approach this from a computational angle; Heidelberg's research profile in corpus linguistics and natural language processing makes it the right institutional home.

I am drawn to Heidelberg University for reasons both academic and practical. It is one of Germany's leading research institutions, and its Computational Linguistics department has a strong international standing in NLP and language modeling. Studying there would also allow me to continue developing my German — not as a credential but as a genuine mode of intellectual and social engagement with the academic environment. My prior experience adapting to a foreign university at the University of Waikato, New Zealand — where I received an A+ in Applied Computing Tools — gives me confidence that I can navigate a new academic system effectively and contribute to the cohort from the outset.

I am applying for the HAUS Scholarship because it directly addresses the financial gap of my first semester as a self-funded recent graduate. The $7,000 award would allow me to establish my academic footing in Heidelberg without the additional pressure of a first-semester resource shortfall. More broadly, the HAUS community and its ambassador program reflect an understanding of study abroad as a sustained relationship between institutions and individuals — a view I share and want to participate in actively, not merely as a recipient but as someone who brings others into the network over time.

My proposed program of study is the two-year MSc in Computational Linguistics. I intend to focus on natural language processing, with a secondary interest in language modeling and multilingual systems. My undergraduate preparation includes Python programming, database management, business analytics, and a capstone in AI for Business Processes — providing a strong computational entry point. My professional experience, particularly two years of applied data analysis at the U.S. Department of Commerce, where I worked with large Census datasets under time pressure to support federal policy decisions, gives me the research independence and rigor that graduate work demands. Long-term, I see computational linguistics as foundational to a career in language technology applied to international or multilateral communication — institutions where the ability to build systems that work accurately across languages is increasingly critical.

As HAUS Ambassador, I would focus on professional and academic audiences in Utah and Washington, D.C., connecting Heidelberg University to American students and professionals who have not yet considered it as a study destination. I would contribute written reports and photographs documenting my experience, actively engage with HAUS chapter events, and serve as a specific and accessible contact point for future University of Utah applicants considering postgraduate study in Germany.
