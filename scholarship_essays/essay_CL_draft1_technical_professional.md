# HAUS Scholarship Essay — Draft 1 (Version 3)
## Program: MSc in Computational Linguistics, Heidelberg University
## Angle: Technical / Professional
## Target: 500–600 words

---

Growing up in a single-parent household in Utah, I didn't have many resources handed to me, but I had books, and that turned out to be enough. I had a speech impediment and ADHD as a child, and for years my teachers weren't sure I would catch up. But I did, and once I could read I read everything. By my teenage years I was working through German poetry from secondhand books with a dictionary and a level of stubbornness that felt natural only because I'd developed it out of necessity. That curiosity about language eventually found a technical direction through my undergraduate studies in Information Systems at the University of Utah, and it's why I'm applying to Heidelberg University to pursue the MSc in Computational Linguistics. I believe this program sits precisely at the intersection of everything I've been building toward.

What drew me specifically to Heidelberg is the work being done there. I've spent time watching Dr. Letitia Pârcălăbescu's YouTube channel, AI Coffee Break with Letitia. She completed her PhD in Heidelberg's Computational Linguistics department, and her ability to make complex ideas in natural language processing both rigorous and genuinely accessible made clear to me that this department produces the kind of researcher I want to become. The department's strength in NLP and language modeling, housed within one of Europe's oldest and most respected research universities, makes Heidelberg the right home for the graduate work I want to do.

My undergraduate degree gave me a strong technical foundation: Python, SQL, database architecture, business analytics, and a capstone in AI for Business Processes. My year in Germany as a U.S. Department of State fellow through the Congress-Bundestag Programme gave me the research motivation. Living with a Gastmutter who spoke only German, I discovered that the most sophisticated translation tools available couldn't capture tone, humor, or the cultural weight a word carries in its context. That gap between what a computational system produces and what a human speaker actually communicates is the problem I want to spend the next two years studying with the rigor it deserves.

I'm also applying for the HAUS Scholarship because I need the support, and I want to be honest about that. My mother raised four children alone after leaving an abusive marriage, working as an accountant to hold everything together. She always told me her one regret was never having the opportunity to study abroad. I'm financing this graduate program independently, without institutional support or family financial backing, and I'm the first person in my family to attempt graduate study in another country. The $7,000 award wouldn't just reduce a first-semester shortfall. It would make possible something my family never had the chance to pursue, and that's not a small thing.

My proposed course of study is the two-year MSc in Computational Linguistics, with a focus on NLP and computational approaches to multilingual and pragmatic meaning. My applied experience at the U.S. Department of Commerce, where I spent two years analyzing large Census datasets to support federal policy decisions, developed the research discipline and tolerance for ambiguity that graduate work requires. Long-term, I aim to work at the intersection of language technology and international governance, where accurate cross-linguistic communication has real institutional consequences.

As HAUS Ambassador upon returning to the United States, I'd bring Heidelberg to audiences at the University of Utah and through the professional networks I built during my Department of Commerce internship and Congress-Bundestag fellowship in Washington, D.C. I'd share the experience specifically and honestly, including what the program demands and what the city actually offers, because I think the most effective advocacy for Heidelberg is a real account from someone who was there.
