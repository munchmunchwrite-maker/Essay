Cover Letter — Deutschlandstipendium Application
MSc Finance and Information Management
Technical University of Munich

Dear Selection Committee,

My path to TUM began in Germany. I spent a year in Berlin as a U.S. Department of State fellow through the Congress-Bundestag Programme, one of 75 Americans selected nationally for a fellowship that included intensive German language training, a semester of graduate-level courses in political science and economics, community volunteering, and a professional internship. That year confirmed two things. One was that I want to build my graduate career in Germany. The other was that the intersection of financial systems, data architecture, and digital transformation is where I want to do the most meaningful work of my professional life. The MSc Finance and Information Management at TUM is designed for exactly that intersection, and I am applying for the Deutschlandstipendium because it would allow me to pursue it with the focus it deserves.

I hold a Bachelor of Science in Information Systems and Accounting from the University of Utah, graduating in April 2026 with a GPA of 3.912 on a 4.0 scale. My technical training included Python programming, database systems, business analytics, cybersecurity management, and AI for business processes. My finance and accounting coursework covered financial reporting, auditing, management accounting, and international accounting. TUM's FIM program's four core areas — Computational Analytics, Quantitative Finance, Information Management, and Financial Management — build directly on this preparation, and TUM's emphasis on research-driven practical relevance aligns with how I've approached applied work.

That applied experience has been consistent with these interests. At the U.S. Department of Commerce I analyzed large Census datasets to support federal policy decisions on pandemic relief allocation, collaborating across bureaus and communicating findings to audiences without technical backgrounds. At Maverik's IT department I conducted Payment Card Industry compliance audits across approximately 100 store locations and developed error-tracking procedures for a point-of-sale system that reduced post-release transaction issues. At Breeze Airways I worked in tax and treasury, supporting indirect tax research and cross-functional financial documentation. Each of these roles required thinking at the intersection of data systems, financial infrastructure, and institutional decision-making, which is the space the FIM program occupies.

Alongside my academic and professional work, I've sustained volunteer and leadership commitments throughout the past three years. At the University of Utah I served simultaneously as Information Systems Ambassador, Marketing Co-Lead on Business Student Government, Eccles Scholar Ambassador, Residential Advisor, and Campus Life Mentor, in addition to directing campus events for ASUU with a $30,000 budget. In the community I've worked as a Disaster Services Coordinator with the American Red Cross, as an Emerging Leaders Board Member with the National MS Society, and as a volunteer at the Umsonstladen Greifswald during my fellowship year in Germany.

I grew up in a single-parent household as the oldest of four children. My mother raised us alone after leaving an abusive marriage, working as an accountant while I worked alongside my studies throughout university to remain financially independent. I'm the first in my family to pursue graduate study abroad. The Deutschlandstipendium would allow me to pursue the MSc FIM without the constraint of concurrent employment, and I intend to make the most of that.

I'm ready for TUM's program, and I'm committed to contributing to its research environment and community.

Sincerely,
Lauren [Last Name]
