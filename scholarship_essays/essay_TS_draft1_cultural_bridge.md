# HAUS Scholarship Essay — Draft 4 (Revised)
## Program: MA in Transcultural Studies, Heidelberg University
## Angle: Cultural Bridge-Builder / Pattern of Engagement
## Target: 500–600 words

---

I did not grow up with much exposure to other cultures. I grew up in the mountains of Utah in a single-parent household, and for most of my childhood the world beyond my immediate community was something I encountered through books. That early habit — reading my way into other people's realities — eventually became something more active: volunteering with immigrants and refugees at the YWCA in Salt Lake City, teaching myself German from poetry I found in secondhand shops, studying abroad at the University of Waikato in New Zealand, and spending a year in Berlin as a U.S. Department of State fellow through the Congress-Bundestag Programme. Cross-cultural engagement has been the consistent thread running through everything I have done. I am applying to Heidelberg University to pursue the MA in Transcultural Studies because I want to examine that thread with the rigour of a graduate discipline rather than simply accumulate more experiences.

Transcultural Studies asks how cultures meet, transform one another, and produce new meanings through encounter — which is exactly the terrain I have been navigating practically for years without the theoretical framework to fully understand it. What brought me specifically to Heidelberg's program is the Heidelberg Graduate School for the Humanities and Social Sciences and its podcast, "Us and Them." Listening to episodes while preparing my applications gave me a sense not just of the school's research priorities but of its intellectual culture — the way it takes questions of belonging, cultural difference, and identity seriously as scholarly problems that require genuine rigor. That is the environment I want to think in.

My undergraduate degree in Information Systems from the University of Utah — GPA 3.912 — gave me analytical tools that transfer directly to graduate humanities work: structured research, data interpretation, and the discipline of communicating complex findings clearly. My coursework in international accounting and Understanding the Global Economy placed institutional systems in cross-cultural perspective. My fellowship internship at a museum in Berlin, where I organized sensitive digital and physical archives in a German institutional context, added hands-on professional experience in cultural work. All of this sits alongside the more personal touchpoints: the halting kitchen conversations with my Gastmutter that taught me how much language is not words but shared reference, or the young girl I tutored at the YWCA who introduced me to Korean culture through games and stories and showed me what genuine cultural exchange can do for a child who is otherwise struggling.

I am applying for the HAUS Scholarship because this degree is not financially easy for me, and I want to be clear about that. I am self-funding my graduate education without institutional support or family financial backing. My mother raised four children alone on an accountant's salary after leaving an abusive marriage, and she always told us her one regret was never studying abroad. The $7,000 award would make the first semester financially stable and allow me to give my full attention to my studies from the outset rather than managing a resource gap. It would also make real something my family was never in a position to pursue, and that carries real weight for me.

My long-term goal is a career in cultural diplomacy or international policy, where deep cross-cultural understanding is the prerequisite for effective work. As HAUS Ambassador, I would bring Heidelberg to students at the University of Utah who may not have considered it and to professional networks in Washington, D.C. that I developed through my fellowship and internships. I would document my time there in writing and photographs, participate actively in HAUS chapter events, and give future applicants an honest and specific account of what the experience offers — because I think that kind of advocacy, grounded in real experience, is what actually makes a difference.
