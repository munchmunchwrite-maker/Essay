Cover Letter — Deutschlandstipendium Application
MSc Finance and Information Management
Technical University of Munich

Dear Selection Committee,

I am applying for the Deutschlandstipendium to support my studies in the MSc Finance and Information Management at TUM. I completed my Bachelor of Science in Information Systems and Accounting at the University of Utah in April 2026, graduating with a cumulative GPA of 3.912 on a 4.0 scale and consistent Dean's List recognition. My undergraduate training covered both the technical and organizational dimensions of the field, including coursework in database fundamentals, Python programming, business analytics, financial and management accounting, auditing, cybersecurity management, and AI for business processes. TUM's FIM program, with its four core areas of Computational Analytics, Quantitative Finance, Information Management, and Financial Management, is a direct extension of this interdisciplinary foundation, and it is the most rigorous environment I have identified for pursuing it at the graduate level.

Throughout my undergraduate years, I held multiple concurrent leadership and mentoring roles at the University of Utah. As Film Director for the Associated Students of the University of Utah (ASUU), I managed a $30,000 annual events budget and designed programming specifically for underserved student populations, including single-parent families and their children. As a Residential Advisor at the Lassonde Entrepreneur Institute, I supported over 50 students annually, with a particular focus on first-year and international students. As a Campus Life Mentor, I guided groups of incoming and transfer students through their first year of university. I also served simultaneously as Information Systems Ambassador and Marketing Co-Lead on the Business Student Government, advocating for students directly to university deans and collaborating with industry partners including Salesforce and Adobe. These roles were not a credential strategy. I held them because I understood that access to mentorship, support, and institutional knowledge is not equally distributed, and I was in a position to provide it.

Beyond campus, I've maintained volunteer commitments throughout the past three years. I've served as a Disaster Services Coordinator and International Services Outreach Team Member with the American Red Cross, as an Emerging Leaders Board Member with the National MS Society, and as a volunteer at the Umsonstladen Greifswald during my year in Germany as a U.S. Department of State fellow through the Congress-Bundestag Programme. Before moving to Germany, I volunteered with the YWCA Utah working with immigrants and refugees, and I've contributed for several years as a Graphic Design Specialist for the Bennion Center, the University of Utah's civic engagement hub.

I want to be direct about my personal circumstances because they are relevant. I grew up in a single-parent household. My mother raised four children after leaving an abusive marriage, working as an accountant to keep our family stable. I'm the first person in my family to pursue graduate study abroad. Throughout my undergraduate degree I worked consistently alongside my studies, including internships at the U.S. Department of Commerce, Maverik's IT department, and Breeze Airways, to fund my education and remain financially independent. The Deutschlandstipendium would allow me to pursue the MSc FIM without the financial pressure of concurrent employment, and that matters significantly for the quality of academic work I'm able to do.

I've spent several years building the technical skills, international experience, and professional judgment that TUM's program calls for. I'm committed to doing the work well, and I'm grateful for the opportunity to demonstrate that.

Sincerely,
Lauren [Last Name]
